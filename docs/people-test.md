# A ten-minute test with real people

Every reviewer of Concord has asked the same question: has a person tried it? This is the smallest test that answers it, and the footage doubles as the opening of the video.

## Who

Five to ten adults. Older relatives and neighbours are ideal. Use the language each person reads best. Concord has 70, so print the cards in that language.

## What to print

1. **The text sheet.** Open https://concord-health.vercel.app, click "Heart medicines" and print the text in the box as plain text (paste it into a document, 12 point).
2. **The cards.** Same sample, pick the person's language, click "Print the cards".

Bring something to count with: a strip of tablets, sweets or beans.

## What to do

Alternate the order so half the people see the text first and half see the cards first.

With each version in front of them, ask the same three questions:

1. "Show me what you take in the morning." They count out the pills.
2. "When do you take the last one of the day?"
3. "Tell me one thing that means you should go back to the doctor."

Do not help or correct. Thank them at the end and show them the right answers.

## What to write down

One row per person:

| Person | Age group | Language | Saw first | Text: Q1 Q2 Q3 | Cards: Q1 Q2 Q3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 to 70 | Telugu | text | right, wrong, right | right, right, right |

Write right or wrong for each question. Report the totals exactly as they come out, for example "with the text, 4 of 6 counted the morning dose right; with the cards, 6 of 6".

## Filming

Ask each person, before you start, whether you may film them and show the clip in a public video. If anyone says no, film only their hands or skip the camera.

Shots worth having:

- A face, reading the text sheet. Hold for three seconds.
- Hands counting out the pills from the card.
- The moment they point at a picture and say what it means.

Hold the phone sideways, near a window, and keep each clip under ten seconds.

## Where it goes

Send me the table and the clips. The totals go on the site, in the write-up and in the judges' notes, and the clips open the video.
